Numbers and Their Forms

One number wears five costumes. By the end you can say all five for any number up to ten: the digit on the door, the counting word, the number in front of a noun, the ranking word, and the weekday hiding it — flat ٥ · five · five rooms · the fifth floor · Thursday.

Built from Units 1–3 (numbers: Unit 1 p.7, Unit 2 p.4; numbers in use: Unit 3 pp.1–2, 4, drills 4, 6, 7) · ≈25 minutes · read after Lesson 3

What is the book's and what isn't

Everything unmarked is on your class pages. Anything tagged beyond the book completes a pattern the book has started — it is there so the pattern makes sense, for recognition; the class will teach it properly when it gets there.

Multi-digit numbers read left → right

Even inside right-to-left text, the digits of one number sit in the same order as in English: ١٠ = 10 · ٢٥ = 25 · ٢٠٢٦ = 2026. The biggest digit is on the left.

رَقْم = a label number

ما رَقْمُ شَقَّتِكَ؟ — ٥.
A flat number, bus number, phone number is a رَقْم, and you read it with the plain counting word: ٥ → خَمْسَة.

Zero is صِفْر beyond the book — you need it the moment someone reads a phone number.

4. Costume 3 — counting things (3–10)

Unit 3's landlord says خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ, not خَمْسَة غُرْفَة. Two things happened. Step through them:

١ خَمْسَة + غُرْفَة

Start with the two words you know: five, room.

٢ غُرْفَة ← غُرَف

Make the noun plural: rooms. (Plurals come word by word — the book gives you this one.)

٣ خَمْسَة ← خَمْسُ

غُرْفَة is a she-noun (it ends in ة) — so the number takes its ة off. Opposites.

٤ خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ

Join them: five rooms. في الشَّقَّةِ خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ — in the flat there are five rooms.

The book's drill 4 gives you the three forms it needs:

في الشَّقَّةِ in the flat
  • ثَلاثُ
  • أَرْبَعُ
  • خَمْسُ
غُرَفٍ rooms

three rooms · four rooms · five rooms.

The rule behind it: opposites

For 3–10, the number takes the opposite gender of the thing counted, and the thing goes plural. Grammars call it polarity. The book only shows she-nouns so far, so you only need the no-ة forms — but here is the whole picture:

counting a…numberexample
she-noun (غُرْفَة، شَقَّة، سَجّادَة)drops its ة ثَلاثُ غُرَفٍ · خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ
he-noun (وَلَد) beyond the bookkeeps its ة ثَلاثَةُ أَوْلادٍ

(أَوْلاد “children” is Unit 2's plural, so you can hear the mirror image with words you own: three children keeps the ة.)

1 and 2 don't play this game beyond the book

“One” and “two” behave like describing words after the noun, agreeing with it — like جَميل in Lesson 3: بَيْتٌ واحِدٌ · غُرْفَةٌ واحِدَةٌ (one house, one room). “Two” uses a special ending on the noun itself, which the course teaches later. For now: only 3–10 go in front.

5. Asking “how many?”

كَمْ takes a singular noun with the ـًا/ـةً ending (drill 7); the answer comes back in costume 3 (drill 4):

ask

كَمْ how many
  • غُرْفَةً
  • سِتارَةً
  • حَمّاماً
  • سَجّادَةً
  • ثَلاجَةً
في الشَّقَّةِ؟

how many rooms / bathrooms are in the flat?

answer

في الشَّقَّةِ خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ.
In the flat there are five rooms.

Singular after كَمْ, plural after the number — the mismatch is the point.

6. Costume 4 — ranking

“Which floor?” is answered with a ranking word: first, second, third… Stack the building from the ground up (Unit 3 drill 6):

١ الدَّوْرُ الأَوَّلُ

the first floor

٢ الدَّوْرُ الثّاني

the second floor

٣ الدَّوْرُ الثّالِثُ

the third floor

٤ الدَّوْرُ الرّابِعُ

the fourth floor

٥ الدَّوْرُ الخامِسُ

the fifth floor — الشَّقَّةُ في الدَّوْرِ الخامِسِ, the flat is on the fifth floor.

Three things to see in that stack:

  • Position. The ranking word comes after the noun, and both carry الـ: الدَّوْرُ الخامِسُ. Same slot as جَميل in بَيْتٌ جَميلٌ.
  • Shape. From 3 upward it is the counting word poured into the mould فاعِل: ثَلاثَة → ثالِث، أَرْبَعَة → رابِع، خَمْسَة → خامِس. “First” and “second” have their own words.
  • Gender. It agrees like any describing word — add ة for a she-noun. No polarity here; that was only for counting.
#countingranking (he-noun)ranking (she-noun)
١واحِدالأَوَّلالأُولى
٢اِثْنانالثّانيالثّانِيَة
٣ثَلاثَةالثّالِثالثّالِثَة
٤أَرْبَعَةالرّابِعالرّابِعَة
٥خَمْسَةالخامِسالخامِسَة

The she-column is the ة rule you already own, applied: الغُرْفَةُ الثّانِيَة the second room. Only “first” is irregular (الأُولى, no ة).

The mould keeps going

السّادِس · السّابِع sixth · seventh — Unit 4 brought these two onto a class page as clock hours, السّاعَةُ السّادِسَة · السّاعَةُ السّابِعَة (six o'clock, seven o'clock; Lesson 4 §5). One catch: “sixth” is سادِس, from an older root, not from سِتَّة.
الثّامِن · التّاسِع · العاشِر eighth · ninth · tenth beyond the book — the same فاعِل shape on the words for 8–10. Recognise these; the class will drill them when it reaches them.

The ranking word tells the time

السّاعَة the hour is a she-noun, so a clock time is the she-column above: السّاعَةُ الواحِدَة · الثّانِيَة · … · السّابِعَة — one o'clock to seven o'clock (Unit 4 p.1, drill 6). Saying at seven needs no preposition: أَذْهَبُ السّاعَةَ السّابِعَةَ, with the ending turned to -a — see the prepositions chapter.

7. Costume 5 — the days of the week

Unit 3's strip of seven names starts on Saturday, the way the working week does in much of the Arab world. But the names count from Sunday: five of them are a number with الـ in front, and الأَحَد is day one.

— السَّبْت

Saturday — “the cessation, the rest day” (the same root as Sabbath): the seventh day, so no number of its own.

١ الأَحَد

Sunday — “the one”. أَحَد is another word for one (you hear it in the shahāda's الله أَحَد).

٢ الإِثْنَيْن

Monday — “the two”. That is اِثْنان in the shape it takes after a preposition.

٣ الثُّلاثاء

Tuesday — “the three”: ثَلاثَة in an old dress.

٤ الأَرْبِعاء

Wednesday — “the four”: أَرْبَعَة.

٥ الخَميس

Thursday — “the five”: خَمْسَة. Same three letters as خامِس, fifth.

— الجُمْعَة

Friday — “the gathering”, the congregational prayer day. Same root as جامِعَة, the university.

Say the week through, Saturday to Friday, and hear the count 1–2–3–4–5 in the middle.
