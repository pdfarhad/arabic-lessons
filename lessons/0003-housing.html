Housing

One win: say where you live, then walk into a letting office and a furniture shop and get what you want — I live in a flat in the University district · I'd like a flat, please · how many rooms? · I want a bed and a curtain.

1. Where do you live?

Unit 3 opens with the course's first I and you verbs. Lesson 2 gave you يـ he / تـ she; now the front of the verb also says I and you (Unit 3, page 1; drills 2–3):

أَيْنَ تَسْكُنُ؟ where do you live
أَسْكُنُ في I live in
  • بَيْتٍ
  • شَقَّةٍ
  • حَيِّ المَطارِ
  • حَيِّ الجامِعَةِ

Where do you live? — I live in the University district. Then the four places: a house · a flat · the Airport district · the University district .

أَ… = I

أَسْكُنُ · أُريدُ
I live · I want

تَ… = you (m.)

تَسْكُنُ · تُريدُ
you live · you want

تـ now does two jobs

تَسْكُنُ was “she lives” in Lesson 2 — and it is also “you (m.) live” here. Identical word; the conversation tells you which. (Only these two persons are in play; the rest of the verb table comes later in the course.)

2. House or flat — and what number?

  • ask هَلْ تَسْكُنُ في بَيْتٍ؟
    meaning

    Do you live in a house?

  • yes نَعَمْ، أَسْكُنُ في بَيْتٍ.
    meaning

    Yes, I live in a house.

  • no لا، أَسْكُنُ في شَقَّةٍ.
    meaning

    No, I live in a flat. — لا is the unit's first “no”.

  • number ما رَقْمُ شَقَّتِكَ؟ — ٥.
    meaning

    What's your flat number? — Five. رَقْم is a label-number (flat 5, bus 9), read with the plain counting word خَمْسَة. The ـكَ “your” is Lesson 1's اسْمُكَ ending on a new noun; شَقَّة opens its ة to ت first.

3. I want… — the object gets ـًا

The letting and furniture dialogues (pages 2–3) run on one verb. Watch the ending of the thing wanted: it takes ـًا (or ـةً on a feminine noun). Say the ending — it's audible, -an:

أُريدُ I want
  • شَقَّةً
  • سَريرًا
  • سِتارَةً
  • فُرْناً
  • ثَلاجَةً
مِنْ فَضْلِكَ please

I'd like a flat, please · I want a bed · a curtain · an oven and a fridge. The shape is all you need for now — the book's drills 1 and 5 are exactly this list.

The shopkeeper asks room by room — ماذا تُريدُ لِـ…؟ “what do you want for…?” (ماذا, not ما, because a verb follows; لِـ “for” glues onto the noun and fuses with الـ into لِلـ):

لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ for the bedroom

سَريرًا وَسِتارَةً
a bed and a curtain

لِغُرْفَةِ الجُلوسِ for the living room

أَريكَةً وَسَجّادَةً
a sofa and a rug

لِلمَطْبَخِ for the kitchen

فُرْناً وَثَلاجَةً
an oven and a fridge

لِلحَمّامِ for the bathroom

سَخّاناً وَمِرْآةً
a water heater and a mirror

4. Renting: rooms and floors

Four lines from page 2 carry the unit's numbers. Learn them as fixed exchanges here; the numbers chapter explains why خَمْسَة became خَمْسُ and where الخامِس comes from.

  • how many كَمْ غُرْفَةً في الشَّقَّةِ؟
    meaning

    How many rooms are in the flat? كَمْ + a singular noun with the ـًا ending.

  • five في الشَّقَّةِ خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ.
    meaning

    In the flat there are five rooms. Number without its ة + plural noun (غُرْفَة → غُرَف).

  • which floor في أَيِّ دَوْرٍ الشَّقَّةُ؟
    meaning

    On which floor is the flat? أَيّ = which.

  • fifth الشَّقَّةُ في الدَّوْرِ الخامِسِ.
    meaning

    The flat is on the fifth floor. “Fifth” is an adjective after the noun, both with الـ.

5. A nice house, a nice flat

The unit's other new shape: a describing word follows its noun and copies its gender — the same ة switch as طالِب / طالِبَة (drills 8–9):

he-noun → جَميلٌ

لَدَيْنا بَيْتٌ جَميلٌ · فُرْنٌ جَميلٌ · سَخّانٌ جَميلٌ

we have a nice house · oven · heater

she-noun → جَميلَة

لَدَيْنا شَقَّةٌ جَميلَة · سَجّادَةٌ جَميلَة · غُرْفَةٌ جَميلَة

we have a nice flat · rug · room

لَدَيْنا is “we have” — the landlord's and shopkeeper's word. Take it as a fixed frame.

6. Fixed phrases of the unit

  • shop أَيَّ خِدْمَةٍ؟
    meaning

    How can I help? — lit. “which service?”; the shopkeeper's opener.

  • admit تَفَضَّلْ، اُدْخُلْ.
    meaning

    Please — come in. تَفَضَّلْ is the all-purpose “go ahead / here you are / after you”; اُدْخُلْ is “enter!”.

  • view أُريدُ مُشاهَدَةَ الشَّقَّةِ.
    meaning

    I'd like to see the flat — lit. “I want viewing of the flat”. Swap the last word: أُريدُ مُشاهَدَةَ الأَثاثِ (the furniture).

  • more? هَلْ تُريدُ شَيْئاً آخَرَ؟ — شُكْراً.
    meaning

    Do you want anything else? — Thank you (= no, thanks). شَيْء thing · آخَر other.

  • some أُريدُ بَعْضَ الأَثاثِ.
    meaning

    I want some furniture. بَعْض + the noun with الـ.

7. The days of the week

Page 4 is a strip of seven names, starting from Saturday — the Arabic week's first day. Five of them hide a number you already know; the numbers chapter shows which.

Saturday · Sunday · Monday · Tuesday · Wednesday · Thursday · Friday

9. Quick check

“I live in a house” is:

  1. تَسْكُنُ في بَيْتٍ
  2. يَسْكُنُ في بَيْتٍ
  3. أَسْكُنُ في بَيْتٍ
  4. أَسْكُنُ في شَقَّةٍ

Which is the correct “I want a bed”?

  1. أُريدُ سَريرًا
  2. أُريدُ سَريرٌ
  3. تُريدُ سَريرًا
  4. أُريدُ سَريرٍ

“We have a nice flat” is:

  1. لَدَيْنا بَيْتٌ جَميلٌ
  2. لَدَيْنا شَقَّةٌ جَميلٌ
  3. لَدَيْنا بَيْتٌ جَميلَة
  4. لَدَيْنا شَقَّةٌ جَميلَة

Which asks “what do you want for the bedroom?”

  1. ما تُريدُ لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ؟
  2. ماذا تُريدُ لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ؟
  3. ماذا تُريدُ لِغُرْفَةِ الجُلوسِ؟
  4. هَلْ تُريدُ لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ؟

Say aloud: “Where do you live?” — then answer “I live in the Airport district.”

أَيْنَ تَسْكُنُ؟ — أَسْكُنُ في حَيِّ المَطارِ.

Say aloud, room by room: what you'd buy for the bathroom.

أُريدُ سَخّاناً وَمِرْآةً لِلحَمّامِ.
