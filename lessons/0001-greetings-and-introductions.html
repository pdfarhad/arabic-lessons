Greetings & Introductions

One win: meet someone, greet them, learn their name and where they're from, and get every word correctly gendered — out loud.

1. The five exchanges

Unit 1 is five question–answer pairs. Say each aloud before pressing 🔊, then compare. This order is the whole conversation the unit builds:

  • greet السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُم — وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلام
    meaning

    Peace be upon you — and upon you be peace. The fixed opener + its fixed reply, whoever you are talking to.

  • name ما اسْمُكَ؟ — اِسْمي خالِد
    meaning

    What is your name? — My name is Khalid. اِسْم name · ـي my · ـكَ your (to a man).

  • state كَيْفَ حالُكَ؟ — بِخَيْرٍ، وَالحَمْدُ لله
    meaning

    How are you? — Fine, praise be to God. Literally “how is your state?” — the answer is a fixed phrase.

  • origin مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ؟ — أَنا مِنْ باكِسْتان
    meaning

    Where are you from? — I am from Pakistan. مِنْ from + أَيْنَ where. Countries: باكِسْتان · تُرْكِيا · مِصْر · سورِيا.

  • check هَلْ أَنْتَ باكِسْتانِيٌّ؟ — نَعَمْ، أَنا باكِسْتانِيٌّ
    meaning

    Are you Pakistani? — Yes, I am Pakistani. هَلْ turns any statement into a yes/no question. It has no translation.

2. The machine under it: who are you talking to?

Every “you” word in Unit 1 changes with the listener. Same letters — the vowel does the work:

To a man → a

أَنْتَ · ما اسْمُكَ؟ · كَيْفَ حالُكَ؟

anta · ends in -ka

To a woman → i

أَنْتِ · ما اسْمُكِ؟ · كَيْفَ حالُكِ؟

anti · ends in -ki

Listen for it

أَنْتَ and أَنْتِ are spelled with the same three letters. In speech only the final vowel separates them — this is why the class drills them as pairs (Unit 1, pages 1–2: the same dialogue twice, once masculine, once feminine).

3. Talking about people: هُوَ، هِيَ and the ة

To describe someone, pick the pronoun, then make the description agree. The feminine is built from the masculine by adding ة — one letter carries all the agreement in this unit (Unit 1, page 8, drills 1–2):

هُوَ he
  • طالِبٌ
  • مُدَرِّسٌ
  • طَبِيبٌ
  • مِصْرِيٌّ
هِيَ she
  • طالِبَةٌ
  • مُدَرِّسَةٌ
  • طَبِيبَةٌ
  • مِصْرِيَّةٌ

student · teacher · doctor · Egyptian — masculine, then feminine.

Nationality words are just country + ـيّ (the nisba ending): مِصْر ← مِصْرِيّ، سورِيا ← سُورِيّ. Add ة and it's feminine: مِصْرِيَّة.

4. Introducing someone

Point with the right “this”, then describe with the right pronoun — the two choices must match the person (Unit 1, pages 5–6):

  • him هَذا أَخي. هُوَ مُدَرِّسٌ.
    meaning

    This is my brother. He is a teacher.

  • her هَذِهِ أُخْتي. هِيَ طَبِيبَةٌ.
    meaning

    This is my sister. She is a doctor. The reply to any introduction: أَهْلاً وَسَهْلاً — welcome!

7. Quick check

You are greeting a woman. “How are you?” is:

  1. كَيْفَ حالُكَ؟
  2. كَيْفَ حالُكِ؟
  3. كَيْفَ حالُهُ؟
  4. كَيْفَ حالُها؟

“She is Egyptian” is:

  1. هُوَ مِصْرِيٌّ
  2. هِيَ مِصْرِيٌّ
  3. هِيَ مِصْرِيَّةٌ
  4. أَنا مِصْرِيَّةٌ

Someone says السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُم. You reply:

  1. وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلام
  2. أَهْلاً وَسَهْلاً
  3. مَعَ السَّلامَة
  4. الحَمْدُ لله

Ask a man where he is from — say it aloud, then check.

مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ؟ — min ayna anta?

Introduce your sister, the student — two short sentences, aloud.

هَذِهِ أُخْتي. هِيَ طالِبَةٌ.
