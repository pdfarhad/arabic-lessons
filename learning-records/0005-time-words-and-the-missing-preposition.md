# Time words join the prepositions chapter, and "no preposition" is taught as a shape

Unit 4 (ingested 2026-09-02) answers مَتى five ways. Three use a little word
(عِنْدَ الفَجْرِ، بَعْدَ الصَّلاةِ، في الصَّباحِ); two use none (السّاعَةَ السّابِعَةَ،
يَوْمَ العُطْلَةِ). User's standing instruction for the chapter: "the chapter will grow"
(learning record 0004); this ingest: "update the preposition chapter as well".

**Decision 1 — عِنْدَ and بَعْدَ go into c002 as prepositions.** Grammarians class them
as adverbs of time (ظُرُوف), "semi-prepositions" on Wikipedia; Wiktionary labels both
"preposition". Both sources were checked (structural claims need more than one). For
the learner the behaviour is identical to في — a following -i — so the chapter files
them with the others, names the grammarians' distinction once in a callout, and does
not drill it. The ledger now has ten words.

**Decision 2 — the bare time ending is taught as "the missing preposition".** English
needs *at* seven / *on* the day off; the book's lines use no word and end the time
word in -a. Taught as a shape (same policy as the -i after prepositions and the ـًا
object ending): "-a on the time word = at/on", with a reveal contrasting السّاعَةُ /
السّاعَةَ and يَوْمُ / يَوْمَ. The chapter says في before a clock time is also heard
and not wrong — an honest note, not a second frame to drill. No case terminology
(MISSION.md).

**Decision 3 — a "beyond the book" tag is lifted in place when a unit attests it.**
c001 tagged 6th–10th ordinals beyond the book; Unit 4 puts السّادِسَة and السّابِعَة on
a class page as clock hours. The tag moved to 8th–10th, a short clock callout was
added, and the knowledge note's ⟂ was narrowed — no restructuring, no new chapter.
Recorded in NOTES.md as the convention.

**Also decided.** The feminine you-form (تَفْعَلينَ) and the future سَـ enter as frames
with their boundary stated (three attested feminine forms; "the ending works on every
verb here"), not as a paradigm — consistent with learning record 0001. The one
pattern-completing form shown in Lesson 4's verb table (تَكْوي) carries the tag.
