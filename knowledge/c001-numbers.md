# Knowledge: Numbers and Their Forms

> Distilled from topic chapter [c001](../chapters/c001-numbers.html). Cross-unit:
> built from Units 1–3, clock hours added from Unit 4 (2026-09-02). "Beyond the book"
> claims are marked ⟂ — recognition only.

- **Claim.** The book uses the Eastern Arabic digits ٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩; multi-digit numbers
  read left-to-right even inside right-to-left text (١٠ = 10, ٢٥ = 25).
  — Unit 3 p.1 (flat ٥, house ٩), drill numbering pp.5; Wikipedia *Arabic grammar*
  § Numerals (digits listed 0–10, read 2026-08-30)
- **Claim.** Counting words 1–10 (citation forms): واحِد، اِثْنان، ثَلاثَة، أَرْبَعَة،
  خَمْسَة، سِتَّة، سَبْعَة، ثَمانِيَة، تِسْعَة، عَشَرَة; 3–10 end in ة. A label-number
  (رَقْم) is read with the counting word (٥ → خَمْسَة).
  — Unit 1 p.7; Unit 2 p.4; Unit 3 p.1
- **Claim.** ⟂ Zero is صِفْر.
  — Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Numerals
- **Claim.** Counting things with 3–10: the number takes the *opposite* gender of the
  counted noun (polarity) and the noun goes plural: ثَلاثُ / أَرْبَعُ / خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ
  (feminine noun → number without ة). The book shows only feminine nouns.
  — Unit 3 p.2 (خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ), p.5 drill 4; Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Cardinal
  numerals ("polarity … ثَلَاثُ فَتَيَاتٍ")
- **Claim.** ⟂ Mirror image: masculine noun → number keeps ة: ثَلاثَةُ أَوْلادٍ
  (أَوْلاد is Unit 2's plural of وَلَد).
  — Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Cardinal numerals; Wiktionary أولاد
- **Claim.** ⟂ 1 and 2 are adjectives following the noun and agreeing with it
  (بَيْتٌ واحِدٌ، غُرْفَةٌ واحِدَةٌ); "two" uses the dual ending on the noun (untaught).
  — Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Cardinal numerals ("The numerals 1 and 2 are
  adjectives. Thus they follow the noun and agree with gender.")
- **Claim.** كَمْ takes a singular noun with ـًا (كَمْ غُرْفَةً؟); the answer uses
  number + plural.
  — Unit 3 p.2, p.5 drills 4, 7
- **Claim.** Ordinals 1st–5th: الأَوَّل، الثّاني، الثّالِث، الرّابِع، الخامِس — adjectives
  placed after the noun, both definite (الدَّوْرُ الخامِسُ / في الدَّوْرِ الخامِسِ); from
  3rd up they are the فاعِل pattern on the number root; they agree normally in gender
  (add ة; 1st feminine is الأُولى), with no polarity.
  — Unit 3 p.2, p.5 drill 6; Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Ordinal numerals; Wiktionary
  أولى (checked 2026-08-30)
- **Claim.** 6th and 7th are now attested, as clock hours: السّاعَةُ السّادِسَة،
  السّاعَةُ السّابِعَة (Unit 4 p.1, drill 6); "sixth" (سادِس) comes from a different, older
  root than سِتَّة. ⟂ 8th–10th continue the pattern: الثّامِن، التّاسِع، العاشِر.
  — Unit 4 p.1, p.4 drill 6 (added 2026-09-02); Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Ordinal
  numerals; Wiktionary سادس، سابع، عاشر
- **Claim.** Clock time is السّاعَة + the feminine ordinal, both definite: السّاعَةُ
  الواحِدَة … السّابِعَة (one to seven o'clock). "At seven" takes no preposition — the
  phrase ends in -a: أَذْهَبُ السّاعَةَ السّابِعَةَ (taught as a shape; detail in c002 §5).
  — Unit 4 p.1, p.4 drill 6; Wiktionary ساعة "hour" (checked 2026-09-02)
- **Claim.** Days of the week (week starts Saturday): السَّبْت "rest" (Sabbath root);
  الأَحَد "the one"; الإِثْنَيْن "the two"; الثُّلاثاء "the three"; الأَرْبِعاء "the
  four"; الخَميس "the five"; الجُمْعَة "the gathering" (root ج-م-ع as in جامِعَة).
  — Unit 3 p.4; Wiktionary السبت، الأحد، الاثنين، الثلاثاء، الأربعاء، الخميس، الجمعة
  (checked 2026-08-30)
- **Claim.** ⟂ The feminine counted forms 6–10 (سِتُّ، سَبْعُ، ثَماني، تِسْعُ، عَشْرُ)
  follow the same drop-the-ة rule; ثَماني and عَشْرُ are irregular in shape.
  — Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Cardinal numerals (10: feminine عَشْرٌ); table only,
  reference sheet row 6–10
