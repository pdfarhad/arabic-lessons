# Knowledge: Prepositions — the placing words

> Distilled from topic chapter [c002](../chapters/c002-prepositions.html). Cumulative:
> covers Units 1–4 and grows with each unit (append, never renumber). "Beyond the book"
> claims are marked ⟂ — recognition only. Unit 4 claims appended 2026-09-02.

- **Claim.** Eight prepositions are attested in Units 1–3: في in · مِنْ from · إِلى to ·
  لِـ for/to · عَلى on · بِـ with/in · مَعَ with · لَدَى at (= have). Of these في, مِنْ,
  إِلى, لِـ and لَدَى occur freely; عَلى, بِـ, مَعَ occur only inside fixed phrases.
  Unit 4 adds عِنْدَ at (the time of) and بَعْدَ after, and makes إِلى and بِـ free.
  — library/readable/lesson-01..04.md (grep 2026-08-30, 2026-09-02); Wiktionary entries
  for each (checked 2026-08-30; عند، بعد 2026-09-02)
- **Claim.** في + place: في الغُرْفَةِ، في بَيْتٍ، في حَيِّ المَطارِ، في الدَّوْرِ الخامِسِ —
  including floors ("on the fifth floor" is في in Arabic).
  — Unit 2 p.3, drill 4; Unit 3 pp.1–2, drills 2–4, 6, 10
- **Claim.** مِنْ + origin: مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ؟ / أَنا مِنْ مِصْرَ; also inside مِنْ فَضْلِكَ
  "please" (lit. from your kindness).
  — Unit 1 pp.3–4, drills 3–4; Unit 3 p.2, drill 5
- **Claim.** إِلى + destination: one attestation so far, هَيّا بِنا إِلى المَسْجِدِ.
  — Unit 2 p.3
- **Claim.** لِـ and بِـ are one-letter prepositions written joined to the next word;
  لِـ + الـ fuses to لِلـ (لِلمَطْبَخِ، لِلحَمّامِ), and لِـ + الله = لله (الحَمْدُ لله).
  — Unit 3 p.3 (لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ، للمَطْبَخِ); Unit 1 p.1 (بِخَيْرٍ، الحَمْدُ لله);
  Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Prepositions (بِـ "with, in, at", لِـ "to, for")
- **Claim.** The noun after a preposition ends in -i: ـِ with الـ (في الغُرْفَةِ), ـٍ
  without (في بَيْتٍ). Taught as a shape only (no case terminology, per MISSION.md).
  Country names are the attested exception: مِنْ مِصْرَ (-a), مِنْ باكِسْتان (no ending).
  — Unit 2 p.3; Unit 3 p.1; Unit 1 pp.3–4; Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Prepositions
  ("a noun following a preposition takes the genitive case")
- **Claim.** Preposition + pronoun suffix forms in play: عَلَيْكُم (على + كم),
  عَلَيْهِ (على + ه), بِنا (بِ + نا), لَدَيْنا (لدى + نا). Same suffixes as on nouns
  (Lesson 2); the full paradigm stays untaught (learning record 0001).
  — Unit 1 p.1; Unit 2 p.2–3; Unit 3 p.2
- **Claim.** Arabic has no verb "to have"; لَدَى + suffix does the job (لَدَيْنا "we have").
  The possessed noun keeps its ordinary ending (لَدَيْنا شَقَّةٌ) because it is not the
  object of the preposition. Only "we have" is attested.
  — Unit 3 p.2, drills 8–9; Wiktionary لدى "at, near, with"; Wikipedia *Arabic grammar*
  § Verbs ("Arabic lacks a verb meaning 'to have'")
- **Claim.** مَعَ is listed among the true prepositions on Wikipedia with the note that
  some grammarians classify it as a noun; taught here simply as "with".
  — Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Prepositions (read 2026-08-30)
- **Claim.** Fixed phrases with a preposition inside: السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُم، صَلَّى اللهُ
  عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ، بِخَيْرٍ، الحَمْدُ لله، مَعَ السَّلامَةِ، هَيّا بِنا، مِنْ فَضْلِكَ، لَدَيْنا.
  — Units 1–3 dialogues (library)
- **Claim.** ⟂ مِنْ takes a helping -a before الـ: مِنَ المَسْجِدِ. ⟂ عِنْدَ + pronoun
  suffix is the other "have" word (عِنْدي); the book has used عِنْدَ only for time so far.
  — Wiktionary من، عند (checked 2026-08-30; عند re-read 2026-09-02: senses "near, at the
  house of; at the time of; expresses possession")

### Unit 4 (appended 2026-09-02)

- **Claim.** عِنْدَ + a moment = "at (the time of)": أَسْتَيْقِظُ عِنْدَ الفَجْرِ. Only the
  time sense is attested; the following noun ends -i.
  — Unit 4 p.1; Wiktionary عند (preposition; sense 2 "at the time of")
- **Claim.** بَعْدَ + an event = "after": بَعْدَ الصَّلاةِ، لا أَنامُ بَعْدَ الصَّلاةِ; the
  following noun ends -i. Bare بعد also reads بَعُدَ "to be far" / بُعْد "distance".
  — Unit 4 p.1; Wiktionary بعد (preposition "after"; antonym قَبْلَ)
- **Claim.** عِنْدَ and بَعْدَ are classed by grammarians as adverbs of time (ظُرُوف) —
  "semi-prepositions" — rather than true prepositions (حُرُوف الجَرّ). Taught here as
  prepositions because the learner-visible behaviour (a following -i) is identical; the
  distinction is named once in the chapter and not drilled.
  — Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Prepositions (true prepositions vs ظُرُوف مَكان وَزَمان,
  re-read 2026-09-02); Wiktionary labels both "preposition"
- **Claim.** في + a stretch of time: في الصَّباحِ "in the morning" — the first time-في in
  the course; same word as في الغُرْفَةِ.
  — Unit 4 p.3
- **Claim.** Two time expressions take no preposition; the time word ends -a instead:
  السّاعَةَ السّابِعَةَ "at seven o'clock" (أَذْهَبُ السّاعَةَ السّابِعَةَ), يَوْمَ العُطْلَةِ "on the
  day off" (مَتى تَسْتَيْقِظُ يَوْمَ العُطْلَةِ؟). Taught as a shape ("-a on the time word =
  at/on"), no case terminology. في before a clock time is also heard and is not
  wrong; the book's line has none.
  — Unit 4 p.1, p.3, p.4 drill 6; Wiktionary يوم (accusative يَوْمَ "on the day of",
  with a Qur'anic example); Wikipedia *Arabic grammar* § Prepositions (semi-prepositions
  are "nouns in the accusative")
- **Claim.** بِـ = "by" for a means of transport, its first free use: بِالسَّيّارَةِ،
  بِالحافِلَةِ. بِـ + الـ keeps every letter (no fusion, unlike لِلـ).
  — Unit 4 p.1; Wiktionary ب "with, by"
- **Claim.** إِلى is productive from Unit 4: أَذْهَبُ إِلى المَدْرَسَةِ + six destinations in
  drill 5, three of them people (إِلى الطَّبيبِ، المُهَنْدِسِ، العَمِّ = "to the doctor's" etc.).
  — Unit 4 p.1, p.4 drill 5
- **Claim.** A definite noun + adjective after في both take -i: في المَسْجِدِ الكَبيرِ.
  — Unit 4 p.3
