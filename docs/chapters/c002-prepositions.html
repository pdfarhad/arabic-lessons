Prepositions — the placing words

One win: put anything anywhere — in the room, from Egypt, to the mosque, for the kitchen — with the eight little words Units 1–3 have already used, and say the ending that follows them.

1. Eight little words, one job each

A preposition is a small word that says where something is, which way it is going, or who it is for. Watch the dot in each picture — that is what the word does. Press ▶ or step with ‹ ›.

في سَعِيدٌ في الغُرْفَةِ

في — in. The dot is inside. Saʿīd is in the room (Unit 2 p.3).

مِنْ أَنا مِنْ مِصْرَ

مِنْ — from. The dot is leaving. I'm from Egypt (Unit 1 p.4).

إِلى هَيّا بِنا إِلى المَسْجِدِ

إِلى — to. Heading there. Let's go to the mosque (Unit 2 p.3).

لِـ ماذا تُريدُ لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ؟

لِـ — for (to). Meant for that place. What do you want for the bedroom? (Unit 3 p.3). It glues onto the next word.

عَلى السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُم

عَلى — on. The dot sits on top. Peace (be) on you — the first thing the course taught you (Unit 1 p.1).

بِـ بِخَيْرٍ · هَيّا بِنا

بِـ — with / in. Holding on to something. “In good health” (Unit 1 p.1), “come on, with us” (Unit 2 p.3). Also glues on.

مَعَ مَعَ السَّلامَةِ

مَعَ — with (together). Two dots side by side. “(Go) with safety” = goodbye (Unit 1 p.5).

لَدَى لَدَيْنا شَقَّةٌ جَميلَة

لَدَى — at (someone's). A box beside a person: “at us is a nice flat” = we have one (Unit 3 p.2). This is how Arabic says have.

3. The two that stick to the next word

لِـ and بِـ are single letters. They never stand alone — they are written joined to whatever follows. Watch what happens when لِـ meets a word that starts with الـ:

١ لِـ + غُرْفَة ← لِغُرْفَةِ

A plain word: لِـ simply joins on. لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ — for the bedroom.

٢ لِـ + المَطْبَخ ← لِلمَطْبَخِ

A word with الـ: the ا of الـ disappears and you write two lams — لِلـ. لِلمَطْبَخِ — for the kitchen. Same in لِلحَمّامِ.

٣ لِـ + الله ← لله

The special case you say every day: الحَمْدُ لله — praise (belongs) to God. Same rule, one more letter dropped.

بِـ means with or in and joins the same way: بِخَيْرٍ “in goodness” is how you answer “how are you”, and بِنا “with us” sits inside “let's go” .

“What do you want for the kitchen?” — spell it right.

ماذا تُريدُ ___؟

لِلمَطْبَخِ · لِالمَطْبَخِ · لِمَطْبَخِ

لِلمَطْبَخِ

لِـ + الـ fuses into لِلـ — the alif goes, two lams stay.

“What do you want for the bedroom?”

ماذا تُريدُ ___ النَّوْمِ؟

لِغُرْفَةِ · في غُرْفَةِ · لِلغُرْفَةِ

لِغُرْفَةِ

“Room of sleep” has no الـ on غُرْفَة, so لِـ just joins: لِغُرْفَةِ النَّوْمِ.

4. Listen to the ending

The word after a preposition changes its last vowel. You have been saying it since Lesson 2 — now hear it on purpose:

١ هَذا بَيْتٌ ← في بَيْتٍ

“a house” ends -un on its own; after في it ends -in. (Unit 3 p.1)

٢ الغُرْفَةُ ← في الغُرْفَةِ

With الـ the ending is a plain -i: في الغُرْفَةِ. (Unit 2 p.3)

٣ المَسْجِدُ ← إِلى المَسْجِدِ

Any preposition, same ending: إِلى المَسْجِدِ، لِلمَطْبَخِ، مَعَ السَّلامَةِ.

! مِنْ مِصْرَ · مِنْ باكِسْتان

The exception you already know: names of countries. مِصْرَ keeps -a, and باكِسْتان / تُرْكِيا / سورِيا take no ending at all. (Unit 1 p.3–4)

The rule, in one line

After any of these little words, the next noun ends in -i: ـِ when it has الـ (في الغُرْفَةِ), ـٍ when it doesn't (في بَيْتٍ). Say it — it is the sound that tells a listener a preposition came first. Country names are the exception you have already met. That is all you need; the grammar behind it comes later in the course.

5. Hidden inside phrases you already say

Six of the course's fixed phrases have a preposition inside. Knowing that turns them from noises into sentences:

you sayinside itliterally
السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُمعَلى + كُمpeace on you (all)
صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَعَلى + ـهِGod's blessing on him
بِخَيْرٍبِـ + خَيْرin goodness = fine
الحَمْدُ للهلِـ + اللهpraise (belongs) to God
مَعَ السَّلامَةِمَعَ + السَّلامَة(go) with safety = goodbye
هَيّا بِنابِـ + ناcome on, with us = let's go
مِنْ فَضْلِكَمِنْ + فَضْلِ + كَfrom your kindness = please
لَدَيْنالَدَى + ناat us = we have

When a preposition meets “you / him / us”

The pronoun becomes an ending on the preposition: عَلى + كُم = عَلَيْكُم, بِـ + نا = بِنا, لَدَى + نا = لَدَيْنا. They are the same endings you met on nouns in Lesson 2 (ـي my, ـهُ his, ـكَ your). Only these four combinations are in play so far — recognise them inside the phrases; the full set comes later in the course.

6. لَدَى — how Arabic says “have”

There is no verb “to have”. The landlord says at us (is) a nice flat, and that is “we have a nice flat” (Unit 3 p.2, drills 8–9):

لَدَيْنا we have
  • بَيْتٌ جَميلٌ
  • فُرْنٌ جَميلٌ
  • سَخّانٌ جَميلٌ
  • شَقَّةٌ جَميلَة
  • سَجّادَةٌ جَميلَة
  • غُرْفَةٌ جَميلَة

Notice شَقَّةٌ keeps its normal -un: لَدَيْنا is already a finished word (لَدَى + us), and the flat is the thing that is at us, not the word after the preposition. Only “we have” is in the book so far.

“We have a nice house.”

___ بَيْتٌ جَميلٌ.

لَدَيْنا · مَعَ · بِنا

لَدَيْنا

“At us is a nice house” → لَدَيْنا. مَعَ needs a noun after it; بِنا is “with us” inside هَيّا بِنا.

9. Quick check

“In the room” is:

  1. في الغُرْفَةِ
  2. إِلى الغُرْفَةِ
  3. عَلى الغُرْفَةِ
  4. مَعَ الغُرْفَةِ

After a preposition, the noun's last vowel is:

  1. -a (ـَ / ـً)
  2. -u (ـُ / ـٌ)
  3. -i (ـِ / ـٍ)
  4. it does not change

“For the kitchen” is written:

  1. لِالمَطْبَخِ
  2. لِلمَطْبَخِ
  3. لِمَطْبَخِ
  4. لَلمَطْبَخِ

Which is the odd one out — the sentence with no preposition hidden in it?

  1. مَعَ السَّلامَةِ
  2. الحَمْدُ لله
  3. هَيّا بِنا
  4. ما شاءَ الله

Say aloud: “Saʿīda is in the prayer room, praying.” — and hear the ending on المُصَلَّى (none: it ends in ى).

سَعِيدَةُ في المُصَلَّى تُصَلِّي.

Which preposition hides in السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُم, and what does the ending كُم add?

عَلى “on” + كُم “you (all)” — peace on you.

Say “we have a nice flat” — and say why شَقَّةٌ keeps its -un.

لَدَيْنا شَقَّةٌ جَميلَة. لَدَيْنا is already complete (لَدَى + us); the flat is not the word after the preposition.
